Results obtained:
Under a 3-synapse threshold and a degree-matched null, 
the sexually dimorphic set reaches fewer distinct downstream neurons than expected, 
particularly at the first two hops. The effect coincides with strong internal connectivity: 6.6% of its outgoing edges stay within the dimorphic set, 
versus about 1% for matched random sets, so its wiring covers fewer unique partners. 

This does not establish that dimorphic neurons are functionally less important; it indicates that this population is locally concentrated rather than broadly projecting. 

Late-hop comparisons are at ceiling and should not be overinterpreted.
