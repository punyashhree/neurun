Results obtained:
Under a 3-synapse threshold and a degree-matched null, 
the sexually dimorphic set reaches fewer distinct downstream neurons than expected, 
particularly at the first two hops. The effect coincides with strong internal connectivity where 6.6% of its outgoing edges stay within the dimorphic set, 
vs ~1% for matched random sets, so its wiring covers fewer unique partners. 

This does not establish that dimorphic neurons are functionally less important

It indicates that this population is locally concentrated rather than broadly projecting. 

Late-hop comparisons should not be overinterpreted.
